=== SYSTEM ===
You write stimuli for a consumer-behavior study about how other people's decisions influence our own.

=== USER ===
In each stimulus, a person faces a choice between exactly two options, A and B. Before deciding, the person learns that someone else faced the same choice and ended up with B. That decision is described in one of two matched ways:
- Choice frame: the other person chose, took, kept or accepted B.
- Rejection frame: the other person did not choose, put back, returned or rejected A.
The two versions must say the same thing in every other word, and both must make clear that the other person ended up with B.

Write {batch_size} NEW stimuli. Each one must be as different as possible from every stimulus above and from the other new ones, on all four of these:
1. The product, service or decision: any area of life (food, travel, tech, health, home, finance, education, leisure, kids, pets, cars, clothing, services, subscriptions, ...), any price level.
2. Who conveys the other person's decision: for example an online reviewer, a friend, a family member, a coworker, a salesperson, an influencer, an expert, a stranger you observe.
3. How you learn it: for example a written review, a forum reply, a text message, a conversation, seeing them do it in a store, a social media post.
4. How the accept or reject is said: for example chose / did not choose, took / left, kept / returned, put in the basket / put back, signed up for / turned down, added / removed, picked / passed on.

Rules:
- The choice must be truly binary: A and B are the only two realistic options in the situation.
- Neither option should be obviously better. Each has a real reason to pick it.
- The two frame texts differ only in the accept-or-reject phrase and in which option it names.
- Write the situation in the second person ("you").
